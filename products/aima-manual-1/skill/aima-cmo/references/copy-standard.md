# The creative standard — what gets approved, what gets sent back

## The opening-frame rule

Ogilvy: five times as many people read the headline as the body; when you've written the headline you've spent eighty cents of your dollar. On social video the headline is the first three seconds. The metric is hook rate. Approve *for* that number: does this frame stop a thumb? A logo doesn't. A person mid-sentence saying something surprising does. Send back any opener that leads with the company.

## Channel changes what's allowed (Schwartz's awareness stages)

- **Meta is interruption.** Nobody opens Instagram looking for a roofer. The reader is problem-aware at best. The ad must name the problem or dream and stop the thumb before it mentions the offer.
- **Google Search is intent.** "Emergency roof repair near me" is solution- or most-aware. Don't convince them they have a problem. Match the search, look credible, make it easy to act.

## Two structures to recognize

- **PAS** — Problem, Agitate, Solve. Most good local ads are PAS.
- **AIDA** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.

## The copy checklist

1. Leads with the customer's problem or dream, not the company name.
2. Follows PAS or AIDA: a hook, a reason to care, a clear ask.
3. Talks about *them*, not *us*.
4. Specific, not vague ("$79 exam", "booked in 48 hours").
5. Has proof: a number, a guarantee, a real review.
6. The CTA matches what actually happens next.
7. Nothing that violates platform policy or the brief's hard rules.
8. Offer in the ad = offer on the instant form = first words of the callback.

## Research the hooks come from

- Five-star reviews of the business for the exact words people use about the dream outcome ("I finally smile in photos again").
- One- and two-star reviews of competitors for the pains people are running from ("waited three weeks and they never called back").
- The Meta Ad Library: an ad a competitor has run for months is probably working. Note offers, angles, formats. That's the market's sophistication: how many similar claims it has already heard.

## Formats by niche

- Trust-driven (dental, med spa, law): creator UGC and street-interview styles read as real people.
- Visual before-and-after (roofing, remodeling, auto detailing, med spa): image ads and product-hype video showing the transformation.
- Fun, high-frequency (restaurants, gyms): mascot vlog, song ad, story ads.
- Explainer for any offer that needs a sentence of setup.

## Ask for a spread

Caples: one ad can sell many times what a near-identical one sells; the game is testing to find the outlier. Don't approve one ad. Several hooks, a couple of formats, image and video. Preview widely, approve narrowly; the real credits go on winners.

## Worked example (dentist)

**Sent back.** Opening frame: the practice logo. Copy: "Smith Family Dental — serving [town] for 20 years. Call us today!" Leads with the company, no hook, no offer.

**Approved.** Opening frame: a real patient mid-sentence, caption on screen: "I hadn't smiled in photos for years." Copy: "Hate your smile? New patients get a full exam, X-rays, and cleaning for $79 this month — most people book same-week. Tap to grab a spot." Problem, dream, speed, offer, ask.

## Google responsive search ads

- 15 headlines (≤30 chars), 4 descriptions (≤90 chars). Google assembles the combination per search.
- Coverage, not repetition: service ("Emergency Roof Repair"), location ("Roofers in [Town]"), offer ("Free Roof Inspection"), proof ("500+ 5-Star Reviews"), CTA ("Call for Same-Day Service"), plus urgency and a question.
- Pin only what must appear. Over-pinning collapses what Google can test.
- Call and location assets on, always, for local.
- Review against the search intent, not against the Meta creative.

## Instant forms

Two Meta form types: **More Volume** (single screen, maximizes count, catches low-intent taps) and **Higher Intent** (adds a review step). Junk leads → switch to Higher Intent and add one qualifying question.
