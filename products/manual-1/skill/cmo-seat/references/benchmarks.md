# Benchmarks, metrics, and the niche quick-reference

Everything here is a median and directional. Sources: WordStream by LocaliQ Facebook Ads Benchmarks 2025 (leads objective) and Google Ads Benchmarks 2026; hook-rate ranges from industry reporting. Say "roughly" and give the year when you quote them. The job is to beat these.

## Metric definitions (define the first time you use one)

- **CPM** — cost per 1,000 impressions. The price of attention.
- **CTR** — click-through rate. Whether the ad is compelling.
- **CPC** — cost per click.
- **CPL** — cost per lead. Usually the headline number for local lead gen.
- **CVR** — conversion rate: of those who clicked or saw the form, the share who became a lead.
- **ROAS** — return on ad spend: revenue ÷ spend. 3.0 means $3 back per $1.
- **Hook rate** — three-second video views ÷ impressions. Meta: ~25% average, 30%+ strong, 40%+ elite, under ~20% means the first frame is being scrolled past.
- **Lead** — a person who gave contact information asking to hear from the business (form fill or call). Not a customer.
- **Speed-to-lead** — how fast a new lead is contacted. MIT/InsideSales (Oldroyd, 2007): calling within 5 minutes vs 30 makes contact 100× more likely and qualification 21× more likely. HBR "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (2011): average response among 2,241 firms was 42 hours; 23% never responded; responding within an hour was ~7× more likely to qualify than one hour later.

## Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin

| Gross margin | Break-even ROAS |
|---|---|
| 25% | 4.0 |
| 30% | 3.33 |
| 35% | 2.86 |
| 40% | 2.5 |
| 50% | 2.0 |
| 60% | 1.67 |
| 70% | 1.43 |
| 80% | 1.25 |

Above break-even is profit. Without margin you can say the business is getting leads, not that it's making money.

## Overall medians

- Meta (leads objective, 2025): CPL ~$27.66 (up ~21% from $22.87), CVR ~7.72%, CPC ~$1.92. Industry spread runs from a few dollars to over seventy.
- Google Search (2026): CPC ~$5.42, CTR ~6.64%, CVR ~8.18%, CPL ~$66.69. Google leads cost more than Meta leads; that is the price of intent.

## Close-rate starting assumptions (replace with the business's own numbers as soon as they exist)

Lead-to-customer, not lead-to-appointment. High-ticket lead gen (roofing, HVAC installs, legal, implants): 10–20%. Low-ticket intro offers (a $79 exam, a gym trial, a restaurant deal): 30–50%. Always say which you assumed and show low / assumed / high.

## Budget floor

For most local lead gen, roughly $50–$100 a day to start, so the platform produces a handful of leads a day and has data to learn from. Tune once CPL is visible against the business's max affordable CPL.
