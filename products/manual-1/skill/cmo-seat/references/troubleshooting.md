# Troubleshooting by symptom, and the order to test in

## Check the non-ad causes first

1. **Pixel red or unhealthy** → stop-work order. Fix tracking before any other decision. Every other number is a lie until it's green.
2. **Nobody calling leads back in minutes** → not an ad problem. Show the timestamps. The campaign can be perfect and the owner will still say "these don't convert."
3. **Ad, form, and callback say different things** → junk leads and rising CPL. Make them match.

## Symptom → likely cause → fix

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| High CPM | Audience too narrow, creative being ignored, or a pricey season | Broaden the audience; get fresh creative in |
| Low CTR / low hook rate | The first three seconds | New hooks and opening frames; lead with problem or dream, not logo |
| Clicks but no leads | Ad and form don't match, or the offer isn't worth the contact info | Tighten the offer, simplify the form, make ad and form say the same thing |
| Leads, but they're junk | "More Volume" instant form catching low-intent taps | Switch to Higher Intent; add one qualifying question |
| Leads, but nobody calls them back | Speed-to-lead | Show timestamps; someone must call within minutes; after-hours coverage |
| Winner slowly dying | Ad fatigue from frequency | Weekly rhythm should have replacements queued before it craters |
| Policy disapprovals | Medical/legal/body claims, before-and-afters that cross the line | Soften the claim, remove the image, resubmit; prevent at review time with hard rules |
| Numbers bounce in week one or two | Learning period | Don't override; it's the testing window you promised |
| CPL creeping up after weeks of stability | Fatigue or seasonality | Feed challengers; check the calendar before scaling |
| Great CPL, owner unhappy | "Lead" was never defined, or capacity is maxed | Re-send the expectations note; check capacity before adding budget |

## What you test by hand, and in what order

The engine optimizes what you gave it. You decide what enters the ring:

1. **Offer** — the biggest lever. If leads are expensive, suspect the offer, not the headline.
2. **Angle / hook** — same offer, different emotional door. This is where hook rate lives.
3. **Format** — image vs video, UGC vs explainer.
4. **Headline** — Ogilvy's eighty cents.
5. **Audience** — only after offer and creative are proven.
6. **Small stuff** — button text, a caption. Last.

Weekly rhythm: same day each week, introduce one or two new variants and retire clear losers. Don't rebuild. Feed challengers to the champion.

## Mistakes that get a CMO fired

- Overriding the rules engine on day three (resets the learning).
- Launching with a weak offer (no creative saves it).
- Not defining "lead" up front.
- Ignoring speed-to-lead.
- Going silent (no weekly report, then "I'm not sure this is working").
- Approving one ad.
- Sloppy credits (unlocking everything).
- Mismatched offer across ad, form, and callback.
- Scaling past capacity.
- Blaming the ads or the business when it's the follow-up or the offer.
